# Number Assurance and Evidence Rules

## Purpose

The portfolio uses complete fictional scenarios to demonstrate decision-making. A correct formula does not make an assumption factual. Every decision number is therefore assigned an evidence class and is kept separate from Anthony Durham's real career results.

## Evidence classes

| Class | Definition | Public presentation rule |
|---|---|---|
| Public benchmark | Dated government or vendor source | Name and link the source; state the limitation |
| Research-based estimate | Best estimate informed by published evidence but not confirmed for the fictional customer | Use for planning and sensitivity only; identify the research basis and required validation |
| Scenario assumption | Fictional workload, price, schedule, or performance input selected to complete the demonstration | Label as assumed; never present it as an achieved result or vendor quotation |
| Derived calculation | Reproducible arithmetic from disclosed inputs | Show the formula and rounding; confidence cannot exceed the input confidence |
| Unknown / pending validation | Price, contract, payroll, workload, or performance fact not yet obtained | Identify the validation owner and do not authorize procurement or scale based on it |

## Accuracy statement

The totals above are arithmetically reconciled across the dashboards and basis-of-estimate documents. They are not asserted as current market bids or customer actuals. Public vendor information supports only the explicitly cited capability or base-price anchors. Real decisions require binding quotations, executed contracts, site surveys, payroll and workload evidence, legal and safety review, and controlled test results.

## Review controls

1. Expand every acronym at first use in each standalone document.
2. Put each financial formula in the business case or basis of estimate.
3. Include a sensitivity that can fail the proposed decision.
4. Keep operational acceptance separate from financial authorization.
5. Re-estimate when scope, site, vendor, contract, labor, volume, or support assumptions change.
6. Run the public validator before publishing; it checks required documents, media, internal links, navigation, public/private boundaries, and stale claims.
