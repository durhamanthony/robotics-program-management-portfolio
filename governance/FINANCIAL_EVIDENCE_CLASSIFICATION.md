# Financial Evidence Classification and Number Confidence

## Purpose

This portfolio uses fictional generic scenarios to demonstrate project and program management. Numbers are included so that decisions can be tested rather than described only with qualitative language. They are planning-grade unless a document explicitly identifies an actual result or binding quotation.

## Evidence labels

| Label | Meaning | Permitted claim |
|---|---|---|
| Public benchmark | A published government, customer, standards-body, research, or vendor source | The source reported the stated value; it does not establish the scenario's actual cost or performance |
| Research-based estimate | A best estimate informed by one or more published references | Reasonable for planning and sensitivity analysis; must be validated for procurement or authorization |
| Scenario assumption | A fictional customer, workload, cost, schedule, or performance input selected to complete the demonstration | An assumed input, never an achieved result or market quotation |
| Derived calculation | Reproducible arithmetic based on identified inputs | Mathematically correct if its inputs are accepted; confidence cannot exceed the confidence of those inputs |
| Unknown / pending validation | Information unavailable without customer data, a time study, engineering evidence, or a supplier quote | An open item that must be resolved before authorization |

## Rules used throughout the portfolio

1. Every business case and Total Cost of Ownership model must identify the source or evidence class of its important inputs.
2. Vendor prices, integration costs, support costs, labor rates, utilization, and performance are assumptions until supported by customer records or binding quotations.
3. Public vendor case studies establish that a workflow or commercial approach may be plausible; they do not prove this portfolio's economics.
4. A derived Return on Investment or payback calculation is not more certain than its least-certain material input.
5. Capacity released is not called cash savings unless a specific expense, overtime payment, contract invoice, or funded position is demonstrably avoided.
6. Unknowns appear as approval gates, not as favorable hidden assumptions.
7. Before a real award, the model must be replaced with customer workload data, site measurements, competitive quotations, contract terms, and finance-approved calculations.

## Reader guidance

The dashboards are intended to demonstrate disciplined decision-making: form a transparent hypothesis, expose uncertainty, test sensitivity, define validation owners, and stop or reshape a program when the evidence does not meet the gate. They are not investment promises, vendor pricing sheets, or audited customer results.
