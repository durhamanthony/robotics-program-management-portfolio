CASE 03 · New Product Introduction

AD-01 — New Robot Through First Sale

HarborPoint Fulfillment · New Product Introduction Program Manager

Closed — limited availability approved18 months$10.975M development program$510K customer five-year TCO
Executive view

Program outcome and acceptance

Amber

Scope

Develop a compact mobile manipulator that moves one approved tote type weighing up to 15 kilograms along an 18-meter indoor route between two fixed stations. The program runs from observed customer workflows through Engineering Verification Test, Design Verification Test, Production Validation Test, first-customer deployment, 30-day acceptance, and a commercial release decision.

Closeout decision

Leadership approved limited availability for the narrow two-station workflow. The first customer accepted the system, Cost of Goods Sold and pilot-build yield met the release gate, and the corrected design exceeded the mission target. General Availability remains gated by actuator second-source qualification and a qualified pipeline that can support the 82-unit development break-even screen.

Decision rule

These are fictional concept-stage inputs, not quoted economics. Customer simple payback is 1.96 years at the documented workload and public labor benchmark. At the $285,000 unit price and $150,000 Cost of Goods Sold ceiling, contribution is $135,000 per unit and 82 units are required to recover the $10.975M development authorization before sales, warranty, support, overhead, working capital, tax, and financing.

Formal closure

Acceptance, handoff, and lessons

ScheduleCompleted at the month-18 release gate
Budget$10,975,000 spent against $10,975,000 staged authorization

Acceptance and handoff evidence

  • Corrected design passed 2,000 representative Design Verification Test cycles at 98.4% mission success
  • Production Validation Test achieved 9 of 10 first-pass units
  • Released Cost of Goods Sold was $149,200 and assembly/test time was 39.4 hours
  • First customer completed the 30-day acceptance window at 98.2% mission success and 95.8% availability
  • Limited availability approved for the common two-station product baseline

Lessons carried forward

  • Narrow scope made safety and value claims testable
  • Serviceability and telemetry must be product requirements
  • A second source needs a dated gate, not a generic mitigation
  • One accepted customer does not establish broad product-market fit
