CASE 01 · Legacy-site retail deployment

Two Humanoids — Retail Backroom Fulfillment

Meridian Department Store · Deployment Program Manager

Executive view

Program outcome and acceptance

Amber

Scope

Deploy two humanoid robots in an unchanged, employee-only department-store backroom. A sales associate submits a stock-keeping unit, size, and color request from a tablet. A robot walks through existing human aisles and doors, uses the short stair route where required, retrieves a shoe box or already-hung garment, and delivers it to an employee handoff point. The same fleet performs put-away and cycle-count work between requests. Tangled hangers, ladders, electrical work, and customer-floor operation remain human tasks.

Closeout decision

The 30-day pilot passed the safety, retrieval, wait-time, inventory-accuracy, damage, and intervention gates, so Meridian accepted the operating procedure and support handoff. Finance did not authorize chain rollout: measured annual gross value was $184,468 versus the $346,000 annual value required for five-year payback at the current planning cost. A second-site decision requires a binding vendor quote and either lower recurring cost or independently measured value at or above $346,000 per year.

Decision rule and confidence limits

There is no positive payback at the pilot inputs. The labor component is 4,965 hours × $26.64 per hour = $132,268, using the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics March 2026 retail-trade total-compensation benchmark. Adding $52,200 of fictional measured margin recovery produces $184,468 annual gross value, below the $218,000 recurring cost. Five-year payback requires $346,000 annual gross value: $218,000 recurring cost plus $128,000 annual recovery of the $640,000 installation. Robot, integration, license, and support prices are planning allowances pending binding quotes.

Formal closure

Acceptance, handoff, and lessons

ScheduleCompleted in week 20 after the 30-day pilot
Budget$640,000 spent against the fictional pilot authorization

Acceptance and handoff evidence

  • Zero recordable safety events and all stop, route-block, network-loss, and human-yield tests passed
  • Retrieval mission success reached 98.1% against the 97% gate
  • Median associate wait fell from a fictional 7.1-minute baseline to 3.4 minutes
  • Inventory location accuracy reached 98.7% and damage or wrong-item rate was 0.2%
  • Employees passed tablet, exception, emergency-stop, recovery, and escalation assessments

Lessons carried forward

  • The human-form-factor rationale must be tied to actual doors, aisles, stairs, shelves, and handoff points
  • Tangled hangers and damaged packaging require an explicit human exception path
  • Operational acceptance and financial approval are separate decisions
  • Do not scale until vendor quotes and measured store value close the $161,532 annual value gap
