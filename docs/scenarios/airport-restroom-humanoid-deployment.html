CASE 05 · Airport facilities deployment

Two Humanoids — Airport Restroom Cleaning Pilot

Pacific Gateway International Airport · Integrated Robotics Program Manager

Phase 1 Amber; Phase 2 RaaS Conditional Green28 weeks including 90-day pilot$1.645M actual / $1.875M authorized$1.270M Phase 2 five-year TCO
Executive view

Program outcome and acceptance

Amber

Scope

Deploy two humanoid robots into four unchanged Terminal 4 public restrooms for controlled routine cleaning and replenishment. The rooms remain closed and verified empty during robot work. Airport custodians retain inspection, prohibited-contamination response, corrective cleaning, supply custody, and reopening authority. The program is managed through three coordinated viewpoints: seller/integrator, humanoid manufacturer, and airport buyer/operator.

Closeout decision

Phase 1 closed Amber: the four-room pilot passed its technical and operating gates but $68,985 of annual capacity value did not support its $3.1345 million five-year capital-purchase path. Phase 2 is a separate Conditional Green recommendation: two robots serve 12 restrooms for 28 productive fleet-hours per day under Robotics-as-a-Service. At $6,000 per robot per month, planning five-year Total Cost of Ownership is $1.270 million, net benefit is $339,650, and Return on Investment is 26.7%. Proceed only after workload validation and binding quotes at or below the $6,595 monthly customer ceiling.

Decision rule and confidence limits

The May 2025 U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics national mean wage for janitors and cleaners is $18.64/hour [PB-H]. The airport's $31.50 loaded task rate is a fictional customer assumption [SA-L], not a BLS value. Phase 1 creates only $68,985 of annual capacity against a $3.1345M capital-path five-year TCO and fails. The separate Phase 2 RaaS case assumes 28 productive fleet-hours/day, $6K per robot-month, $150K integration, and $80K annual retained human support. It produces $339,650 five-year net benefit, 26.7% ROI, about $241,006 NPV at 8%, and 1.53-year simple payback [DC-L]. Break-even is 22.1 productive hours/day; at 22 hours the case is slightly negative, and at the public mean wage even 28 hours/day does not cover the modeled annual run cost. Capacity is not cash savings; workload, prices, performance, and realization remain [UPV].

Formal closure

Acceptance, handoff, and lessons

ScheduleCompleted in week 28 after the 90-day pilot
Budget$1,645,000 actual against $1,874,500 authorization; $229,500 returned

Acceptance and handoff evidence

  • 1,048 of 1,080 scheduled routine missions completed
  • 248 of 270 sampled missions passed the twelve-point inspection on first review
  • All 60 witnessed safety/recovery tests and 24 prohibited-exception drills passed
  • Seller deliverables, manufacturer pilot baseline, airport runbook, training, spares, support, and data handoff accepted
  • No labor savings booked and no airport-wide expansion authorized

Lessons carried forward

  • Human-form-factor rationale must connect to existing doors, partitions, fixtures, tools, and human-height work
  • Seller delivery, manufacturer release, and buyer operational acceptance are separate decisions
  • A safe exception path is part of the product and operating design
  • A technically successful pilot can still fail the scale-economics gate
  • Percentages need denominators, and planning prices need quotations before procurement
