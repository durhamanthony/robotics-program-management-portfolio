Statement of Work — Five-Humanoid Warehouse Deployment

Purpose and assumptions

Configure and deploy five vendor-supplied humanoid robots at one distribution site for a controlled outbound workflow: receive approved cartons from five conveyor lanes, carry them to a floor pallet, build a seven-carton demonstration stack, pause the robots, transfer the pallet into an outbound truck with a robot-operated forklift, return the empty forklift, place a replacement pallet, and restart. The customer provides approved facilities, network access, representative cartons, operational staff, and timely decisions.

Deliverables and acceptance

Deliverable Supplier responsibility Customer responsibility Acceptance evidence
Validated use case Define supported carton/trailer/flow envelope Provide representative process data and samples Signed use-case crosswalk
Five configured robots Build, configure, identify, test, and ship Provide receiving and secure staging Configuration records and FAT (Factory Acceptance Test)
Site/integration design Power, charging, network, data, workflow, controls Approve facility and IT/security changes Approved design baseline
Commissioned operating cell Install, calibrate, integrate, and execute SAT (Site Acceptance Test) Provide access, operators, EHS (Environment, Health, and Safety), and WMS (Warehouse Management System)/conveyor subject-matter experts SAT and safety evidence
Training and support Deliver role training, spares, portal, escalation Ensure attendance and local ownership Assessments and support drill
Pilot and handoff Run controlled pilot and hypercare Operate within approved envelope UAT (User Acceptance Testing), KPI (Key Performance Indicator) window, handoff signoff

Commercial controls

  • Fixed-fee deliverables: design package, standard configuration, FAT (Factory Acceptance Test), SAT (Site Acceptance Test), training, and handoff.
  • Time-and-materials or change order: facility rework, new interfaces, unsupported carton classes, additional shifts/sites, or customer-caused delays.
  • Invoice gates: kickoff, design baseline, FAT (Factory Acceptance Test)/ship, SAT (Site Acceptance Test), and production acceptance.
  • Warranty start, service entitlement, exclusions, travel, spare ownership, and cure periods must be explicit in the executed agreement.

Change and completion

Any baseline change is logged, impact-assessed, and approved before work proceeds. Completion requires deliverable acceptance, open-item disposition, asset/configuration records, training completion, support ownership, final financial reconciliation, and sponsor/customer signoff.
