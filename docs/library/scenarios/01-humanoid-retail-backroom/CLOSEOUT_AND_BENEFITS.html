Closeout and Benefits Report — Retail Backroom Humanoid Pilot

Customer: Meridian Department Store, Store 214 (fictional)
Close date: Week 20
Operational decision: accepted
Chain-rollout decision: held at financial gate

Acceptance results

Measure Gate Fictional result Status
Retrieval mission success at least 97% 98.1% pass
Median request-to-handoff no more than 4.0 minutes 3.4 minutes pass
Inventory location accuracy at least 98% 98.7% pass
Interventions no more than 3/100 2.2/100 pass
Damage/wrong item no more than 0.5% 0.2% pass
Recordable safety events zero zero pass
Annual gross value at least $346,000 $184,468 fail

Financial reconciliation

Installed pilot spending was $640,000 in the fictional budget. Annual recurring planning cost is $218,000. Annualized value is $132,268 from 4,965 returned hours at the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics $26.64 retail-trade compensation benchmark plus $52,200 fictional measured margin recovery. Gross value is $33,532 below recurring cost and $161,532 below the five-year annual threshold. Five-year shortfall is $807,662.

Finance therefore recorded no chain rollout. No savings were booked from returned time because actual payroll, overtime, vacancies, schedules, and customer transaction effects were not supplied.

Handoff and closure evidence

  • configuration, route, item envelope, test records, defect list, support cases, KPI query, and pilot dataset archived;
  • training, support severity, field recovery, security access, privacy deletion, and rollback drills accepted;
  • unsupported-item, blocked-route, and financial risks assigned to named operational owners;
  • contracts, invoices, warranties, assets, licenses, and decommissioning obligations identified; and
  • project team released after sponsor signoff.

Lessons

  1. A humanoid justification must identify the human infrastructure actually used: doors, stairs, aisles, shelves, garment rails, and handoff points.
  2. Tangled hangers and damaged packaging belong in a defined exception workflow until tested evidence says otherwise.
  3. Operational success is not a return-on-investment result.
  4. Planning allowances must not be displayed as market prices.
  5. A program manager adds credibility by stopping rollout when the evidence fails the decision threshold.
