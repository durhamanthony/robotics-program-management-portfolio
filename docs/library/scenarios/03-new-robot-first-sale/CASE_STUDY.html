Case Study 3 — Develop a New Robot Through First Sale

Scenario

A robotics company decides to develop AD-01, a compact mobile manipulator that carries a lidded tote weighing up to 15 kilograms along an 18-meter indoor route between two fixed workstations. Release 1 is limited to flat floors, two mapped stations, 16 operating hours per day, and supervised exception recovery. The program manager coordinates product, systems, hardware, firmware, software, perception, safety, manufacturing, supply chain, quality, service, sales, legal, and the first customer.

Outcome

Move from validated customer problem to a manufacturable, serviceable, supported product and complete the first contracted customer deployment with transparent limitations and acceptance evidence.

Strategy

Start narrow: one payload envelope, two workstation types, controlled indoor floor, supervised operation, and defined exception handling. Retire the highest safety, technical, supplier, and service risks before expanding scope.

Program success measures

  • At least three design partners validate the problem and workflow.
  • Product requirements are baselined, testable, and traced to verification.
  • No open critical safety/security issue at shipment.
  • EVT (Engineering Verification Test), DVT (Design Verification Test), and PVT (Production Validation Test) gate evidence is approved.
  • Released unit COGS (Cost of Goods Sold) is no more than $150,000, PVT first-pass yield is at least 90%, and final assembly/test time is no more than 40 labor hours.
  • First customer site, training, support, spares, and acceptance are ready before shipment.
  • Customer acceptance passes within the contracted cure window.
  • First 30 days achieve at least 95% scheduled availability, 98% mission success, and no more than 3 interventions per 100 missions.

Integrated workstreams

Product/customer; systems/requirements; mechanical/electrical; firmware/software/perception; safety/cybersecurity; test/reliability; supply chain/suppliers; manufacturing/quality; service/support; sales/contracts/customer deployment; finance/program controls.

Major gates

Discovery -> Concept -> Architecture/requirements baseline -> EVT (Engineering Verification Test) -> DVT (Design Verification Test) -> PVT (Production Validation Test) -> First-customer readiness -> Ship/deploy -> Acceptance -> GA (General Availability) decision.

Concrete funding decision

The 18-month NPI (New Product Introduction) plan is $10.975 million, including a 20-person average team, prototypes, tooling, supplier qualification, external testing, first-customer deployment, and management reserve. At a $285,000 selling price and $150,000 COGS (Cost of Goods Sold), the $135,000 unit contribution requires approximately 82 units to recover the development investment before sales, corporate overhead, working capital, and tax. Funding is released by gate; GA (General Availability) is not approved unless the commercial pipeline and manufacturing plan support that volume without repeated customer-specific engineering.
