New Product Introduction Stage-Gate Plan

Discovery gate — end of month 2

Pass only with twelve observed customer workflows, at least four qualified design partners describing the same tote-transfer problem, a measured time/labor baseline, a $330,000 initial-price test, and a named owner for each of the ten highest risks.

Concept gate — end of month 4

Pass only when a feasibility rig moves the 15-kilogram tote through the critical grasp/lift/place sequence; the 18-meter route, interfaces, preliminary hazard analysis, target Cost of Goods Sold (COGS), supplier-risk map, service concept, $10.975 million plan, and stop conditions are approved.

Engineering Verification Test (EVT) gate — end of month 8

  • Two integrated engineering prototypes demonstrate navigation, grasp, 15-kilogram transfer, placement, charging, telemetry, and approved safe states.
  • The top five technical risks have quantitative test results and approved residual-risk actions.
  • The system baseline, interfaces, Preliminary Hazard Analysis, and Design Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (DFMEA) have named owners and verification methods.
  • The updated COGS forecast is at or below $180,000 and all long-lead parts have a supply plan.

Design Verification Test (DVT) gate — end of month 12

  • At least 2,000 representative missions achieve at least 98% success and at least 40 transfers per hour in the two-hour rate test.
  • All mandatory safety, fault, electrical, Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC), cybersecurity, update/rollback, and serviceability tests pass or have an approved non-ship-blocking disposition.
  • Battery, compute, and gripper Field-Replaceable Unit (FRU) demonstrations each complete within 60 minutes including return-to-service.
  • Critical suppliers are qualified or have an approved alternate/recovery plan; forecast COGS is at or below $165,000.
  • Design freeze exceptions identify requirement, hazard, manufacturing, service, schedule, cost, and customer impacts.

Production Validation Test (PVT) gate — end of month 15

  • Ten units are built on the intended process, tooling, suppliers, workforce, and end-of-line test flow.
  • First-pass yield is at least 90%, final assembly/test is no more than 40 labor hours per unit, and actual/committed COGS is at or below $150,000.
  • Every unit has serial, hardware, software, calibration, test, and deviation traceability.
  • Field tools, spares, Return Material Authorization (RMA), support knowledge, training, telemetry, on-call, packaging, shipping, and first-customer readiness all pass their checklists.

First-customer ship gate — month 16

No open ship-stopping safety/security defect; approved configuration; contract/acceptance crosswalk; site and support ready; customer trained; spares and field coverage positioned; cutover/rollback and communications approved; known limitations disclosed and accepted.

First-customer acceptance and General Availability (GA) decision — end of month 18

Pass the 30-day window only with at least 98% mission success, at least 95% scheduled availability, no more than three interventions per 100 missions, zero unresolved Severity-1 safety/security defects, trained users, completed support drill, and customer confirmation of the measured workflow baseline. Then approve GA, narrow the supported envelope, extend the controlled pilot, or stop.
