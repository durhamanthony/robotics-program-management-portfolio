Airport Owner / Operator Implementation Plan

Accountable outcome

Introduce the pilot without degrading passenger service, worker safety, accessibility, privacy, cleaning quality, or business continuity; accept only evidence-backed capability; and retain authority over daily operation and expansion.

Airport workstreams

  1. Need and baseline: document routine service, exceptions, staffing, closure time, complaints, quality, and cost.
  2. Procurement: separate mandatory requirements, evaluated options, contract deliverables, acceptance, support, data, insurance, and exit rights.
  3. Facilities and terminal operations: approve room selection, charger/supply location, entry controls, egress, power, network, and recovery route.
  4. Workforce and change: involve custodians and representatives in workflow, hazard analysis, inspection, training, and benefits definition.
  5. Safety and health: exposure determination, chemical program, prohibited material, personal protective equipment, spill, sharps, and emergency procedures.
  6. Cybersecurity and privacy: identity, segmentation, logging, purpose, retention, access, incident response, and vendor remote support.
  7. Operational readiness: staffing, room closure, daily inspection, supplies, fallback, escalation, and reopening authority.
  8. Acceptance and benefits: independent evidence review, cost reconciliation, measured capacity, service quality, and expansion decision.

Daily authority

The Airport Duty Manager may suspend the pilot. The custodian controls room reopening. Airport Safety controls biohazard procedure. Cybersecurity controls remote access. The seller cannot add tasks or change data use without airport approval.

Completed result

Airport operations accepted the controlled pilot and operating handoff. It did not authorize workforce reduction or airport-wide rollout. The airport approved a 60-day evidence extension only within the existing rooms, hours, chemicals, staffing, and support controls.
