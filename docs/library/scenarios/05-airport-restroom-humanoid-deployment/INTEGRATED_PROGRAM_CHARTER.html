Integrated Program Charter — Airport Restroom Humanoid Pilot

Authorization

Pacific Gateway International Airport authorizes a $1,874,500 controlled pilot to determine whether two humanoid robots can perform routine cleaning and replenishment in four existing Terminal 4 restrooms while preserving human safety, service quality, workforce authority, and the current physical layout.

Business problem

Public restrooms require frequent, repetitive fixture and floor service during continuous airport operations. Open-floor machines address only part of the work. Permanent retrofit would disrupt passenger facilities that remain designed for people. The airport needs evidence—not a presumption—on whether a human-scale robot can cover a broader routine task set without construction and without transferring biohazard, passenger-protection, or quality decisions away from trained people.

Objectives and acceptance thresholds

Objective Measure Threshold Evidence owner
Operate safely in closed rooms Witnessed entry, stop, geofence, network-loss, leak, and recovery tests 60 of 60 pass; zero uncontrolled public entries Airport Safety
Complete routine missions Completed scheduled missions At least 97% of 1,080 Integrator
Meet cleaning inspection Twelve-point first-pass inspections At least 90% of sampled missions Custodial Quality
Protect exception handling Sharps/body-fluid/chemical/standing-water drills 24 of 24 safe stop and human escalation Airport Operations
Support the system Critical acknowledgement and field restoration drills Acknowledge within 15 minutes; restore or safe-plan within 4 hours Seller Support
Establish economics Reconciled actuals, measured labor time, binding scale quotations Steering-committee signoff; no unresolved arithmetic Airport Finance

Scope included

Robot and dock delivery; site survey; room mapping; routine task configuration; approved-tool interfaces; network and identity; entry controls; privacy review; safety analysis; Factory Acceptance Test; Site Acceptance Test; training; 30-day shadow mode; 90-day controlled pilot; support; inspection; benefits measurement; handoff; and closeout.

Scope excluded

Construction, public co-occupancy, biohazard remediation, sharps pickup, electrical or plumbing work, facial recognition, law enforcement, employee displacement commitments, autonomous chemical selection, generalized manipulation, and use outside the four approved rooms.

Governance

  • Executive Steering Committee: airport sponsor, seller executive, manufacturer executive, airport safety, labor/operations, and finance.
  • Integrated Program Manager: owns the master plan, change control, cross-party dependencies, decision log, and consolidated status.
  • Three Project Managers: seller/integrator; manufacturer; airport owner/operator.
  • Acceptance authority remains with the airport. Product release authority remains with the manufacturer. Contract delivery accountability remains with the seller.

Stage gates

  1. Charter and funding.
  2. Site and workflow baseline.
  3. Design and hazard review.
  4. Factory Acceptance Test.
  5. Site Acceptance Test and training.
  6. Shadow-mode exit.
  7. Pilot acceptance.
  8. Handoff, commercial decision, and closeout.

Closeout

The pilot can close successfully even if scale is not authorized. Successful project closeout requires accepted deliverables, reconciled cost, controlled residual risks, named operating owners, retained evidence, and a documented decision on expansion.
