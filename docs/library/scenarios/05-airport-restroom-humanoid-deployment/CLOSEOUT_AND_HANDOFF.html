Closeout and Handoff — Airport Restroom Humanoid Pilot

Closure statement

The Executive Steering Committee closed the 28-week implementation and 90-day pilot after accepting contracted deliverables, safety and quality evidence, cost reconciliation, support readiness, operating ownership, and residual-risk transfer.

Handoff package

  • Serialized asset and configuration record.
  • Approved room, route, fixture, tool, and chemical baseline.
  • Daily runbook, exception matrix, inspection, and manual fallback.
  • Safety, chemical, privacy, cybersecurity, and accessibility approvals.
  • Support severity, contacts, spare kit, warranty, and remote-access procedure.
  • Training records and role assessments.
  • Mission, inspection, fault, intervention, support, cost, and benefit dataset.
  • Residual risk and product-gate register.

Financial closeout

Actual cost was $1,645,000 against $1,874,500 authorization. Finance returned $229,500. No labor saving was booked. The $68,985 annualized capacity value remains a planning benefit subject to continuing measurement.

Residual decisions

The airport accepted no more than a 60-day controlled extension in the same four rooms. The seller may support but not expand scope. The manufacturer may continue product development but may not present the pilot release as General Availability. Any scale proposal returns to procurement, safety, workforce, cybersecurity, privacy, operations, and finance gates.

Lessons

  1. Human-form-factor rationale must be tied to physical tasks and existing-room constraints, not appearance.
  2. A safety exception is part of the designed workflow, not a robot failure to hide.
  3. Quality sampling needs denominators, defect categories, and named inspection authority.
  4. A technically accepted pilot can still produce a “do not scale” financial decision.
  5. Seller delivery, manufacturer release, and airport operational acceptance require separate accountable owners.
