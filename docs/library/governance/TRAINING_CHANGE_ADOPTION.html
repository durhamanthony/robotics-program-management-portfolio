Training, Change, and Adoption Plan

Audiences and outcomes

Audience Must be able to do
Operators Start/stop missions, recognize operating states, maintain zones, perform approved recovery, escalate safely
Supervisors Manage work allocation, performance, staffing, exceptions, and go/no-go decisions
Frontline support Verify entitlement, collect evidence, use known-error guidance, route by severity
Field service Isolate energy, diagnose authorized subsystems, replace/calibrate approved units, document return to service
Engineering/L3 Analyze logs, reproduce defects, authorize technical changes, publish corrections
Safety/security/site teams Audit controls, respond to events, approve changes within authority

Deliverables

Role matrix; learning objectives; training deck; demonstrations; hands-on lab; knowledge check; practical assessment; attendance; certification/authorization record; refresher plan; job aids; FAQ; support contacts.

Adoption approach

  1. Identify affected roles, current workflow, concerns, incentives, and labor/change constraints.
  2. Explain why the change is occurring and what will remain under human authority.
  3. Involve operators in site survey, workflow validation, exception design, and UAT.
  4. Pilot with champions and capture usability/intervention feedback.
  5. Publish shift-ready job aids at the point of work.
  6. Measure use, interventions, retraining, satisfaction, and workaround behavior.

Readiness gate

Launch requires trained named operators and supervisors on every covered shift, support contacts tested, assessments passed, local-language materials where required, and a documented path for untrained/temporary personnel.

Reinforcement

Review at 7, 30, and 60 days; update materials from incidents and feedback; retrain after material workflow, safety, UI, or configuration changes.
