Values are illustrative full-time-equivalent ranges and must be recalculated from actual complexity, geography, shift coverage, installed base, travel, and supplier responsibilities.

Capacity method

  1. Estimate demand by phase, site, robot count, shift, severity, and planned work.
  2. Separate productive hours from meetings, travel, training, leave, and on-call recovery.
  3. Apply skill constraints: safety-authorized, electrical, mechanical, software, customer access, and regional requirements.
  4. Compare demand with named capacity by week and identify over-allocation.
  5. Resolve through scope sequencing, cross-training, vendor capacity, schedule change, or approved additional staffing.

Launch coverage

  • Name primary and backup owners for deployment, safety, software, hardware, network, customer operations, and communications.
  • Establish 24x7 coverage only where contract/launch risk requires it; document rotations and compensatory rest.
  • Confirm travel, site access, tools, spares, and remote access before assigning a person as available.

Capacity risks

Single points of knowledge, competing launches, supplier travel constraints, delayed background/site access, burnout from prolonged hypercare, and engineering teams consumed by support incidents are tracked in RAID with mitigation dates.
