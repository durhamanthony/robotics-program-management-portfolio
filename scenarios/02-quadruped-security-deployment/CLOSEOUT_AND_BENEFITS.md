# Closeout and Benefits — Quadruped Night Security

**Customer:** Sentinel Ridge Industrial Campus  
**Program duration:** 20 weeks plus a 30-night pilot  
**Closeout decision:** Pilot accepted for two recurring quadruped patrol posts with one onsite human responder retained  
**Financial authorization / final spend:** $730,250 / $730,250

The pilot included daylight and night route tests, stairs, curbs, gravel, wet-route inspection, low light, loss of network, degraded sensor input, docking failure, manual retrieval, and human alert disposition. Robots observed and reported; authorized security staff retained all dispatch and incident decisions.

## Handoff and administrative closure

- Approved routes, geofences, exclusion zones, software versions, docks, and wireless coverage records were transferred to Security Operations.
- Privacy masks, purpose limitations, retention, deletion, access roles, and audit evidence were accepted by Privacy and Legal.
- Security operators, responders, and field-support personnel completed role qualification and escalation drills.
- Reserve coverage, spare charger, manual patrol fallback, retrieval equipment, and on-call ownership are active.
- Residual weather and privacy-control monitoring were transferred to named operational owners.

## Benefits decision

The base case is valid only while two contracted walking-rover posts are removed and one onsite responder remains. That configuration produces a modeled 3.22-year simple payback. If only one rover post can be removed, modeled payback becomes 11.73 years and the expansion gate fails.

## Lessons and expansion gate

1. Survey routes at the actual patrol time and expected weather.
2. Tune and measure operator false-alert workload before unattended patrol.
3. Design evidence retention and support correlation as one workflow.
4. Keep human authority explicit in every alert and dispatch procedure.
5. Return any new route, sensor, schedule, or autonomy level to policy, privacy, safety, network, support, and financial review.
