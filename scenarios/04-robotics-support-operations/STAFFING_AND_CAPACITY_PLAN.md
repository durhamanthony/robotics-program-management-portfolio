# Staffing and Capacity Plan — Robotics Support

## Demand model

Monthly workload = installed robots × contacts/robot + telemetry-created actionable incidents + planned maintenance + deployments/updates + field work + problem/knowledge/admin demand. Segment by model, site maturity, version, severity, geography, skill and coverage window.

## Initial 15-person launch team

| Role | Initial coverage assumption | Capacity trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Service owner/program manager | 1 | Additional product/region or material roadmap load |
| Level 1 (L1) agents | 3; business hours plus contracted on-call | Queue forecast above 75% usable capacity for six weeks or repeated Service-Level Agreement misses |
| Level 2 (L2) robotics support | 4; primary/secondary rotation | Concurrent Severity-1/Severity-2 risk, diagnostic hours, launch load |
| Field service | 4; regional employee/partner mix | More than 32 two-day visits/month, excess travel, or first-time-fix decline |
| Reliability/problem | 1 | Repeat incidents, product maturity, installed base |
| Knowledge/tools/data analyst | 1 | Content debt, automation, and reporting backlog |
| Parts/Return Material Authorization coordinator | 1 | Shipments, depots, no-fault-found, and inventory value |

## Capacity controls

Forecast 13 weeks and 12 months; maintain coverage/skills matrix and on-call sustainability; reserve launch surge capacity; define partner qualifications; cross-train critical skills; cap sustained planned utilization at 75%; measure non-case work; and review regional travel and part locations. See [Operating Baseline and Cost Assumptions](OPERATING_BASELINE_AND_COST_ASSUMPTIONS.md) for workload and cost math.
