# Robotics-as-a-Service Pricing and Unit Economics

> **Evidence-confidence key:** [PB-H] public benchmark or authoritative source/high confidence; [RBE-M] range-based estimate/medium confidence; [SA-L] scenario assumption/low confidence; [DC-L] disclosed derived calculation/confidence inherits the weakest input; [UPV] unverified production value/block commitment until approved.


## Purpose

This artifact tests whether a Robotics-as-a-Service (RaaS) structure can be affordable to the airport and financially plausible for a provider. It does not quote or represent any robotics company.

## Customer price sensitivity

Assumptions held constant: two robots, 60 months, $150,000 initial integration, $400,000 retained human support, and $1,609,650 five-year task-capacity value.

**Table 1. Customer price sensitivity — Evidence: disclosed row/source notes; Confidence: see evidence key and row/source notes**

| Monthly price per robot | Five-year subscription | Five-year TCO | Net benefit | ROI |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $5,750 | $690,000 | $1,240,000 | $369,650 | 29.8% |
| **$6,000 base** | **$720,000** | **$1,270,000** | **$339,650** | **26.7%** |
| $6,595 ceiling | $791,400 | $1,341,400 | $268,250 | 20.0% |

The $6,595 row differs by $25 from the exact mathematical 20% ceiling because the monthly rate is rounded to whole dollars.

## Utilization protection

**Table 3. Utilization protection — Evidence: disclosed row/source notes; Confidence: see evidence key and row/source notes**

| Productive fleet-hours/day | Five-year customer net benefit | Customer ROI | Commercial interpretation |
|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 28.0 | $339,650 | 26.7% | Base planning case |
| 24.0 | $109,700 | 8.6% | Fails 20% target |
| 22.1 | Approximately $0 | 0% | Break-even only |
| 22.0 | ($5,275) | Negative | Stop or redesign |

The base offer therefore needs both a price corridor and a workload floor. A price below the provider floor is not sustainable; a price above the customer ceiling or utilization below 22.1 hours/day is not affordable.

## Commercial decision rule

Award only when the negotiated rate is at or below the customer's ceiling and at or above the provider's approved floor. If the ranges do not overlap, reduce integration burden, increase verified productive utilization, add approved adjacent tasks, change risk allocation, or stop the procurement.
