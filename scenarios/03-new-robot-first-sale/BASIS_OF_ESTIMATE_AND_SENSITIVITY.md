# Basis of Estimate and Sensitivity — AD-01 New Product Introduction

## Estimate class and evidence boundary

The development budget is a fictional concept-stage planning estimate. It is suitable for a portfolio gate discussion, not for a purchase order or financial forecast. Public labor benchmarks only test whether the blended team allowance is directionally plausible; they do not price a robotics program. Supplier quotations, payroll data, prototype designs, certification plans, and customer contracts are still required.

## Customer economics

- Customer initial outlay = $285,000 product + $45,000 installation and training = **$330,000**.
- Gross annual customer value = **$204,019**, calculated from the documented handler-capacity model.
- Annual service = **$36,000**.
- Net annual customer value = $204,019 − $36,000 = **$168,019**.
- Simple payback = $330,000 ÷ $168,019 = **1.96 years**.
- Five-year Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) = $330,000 + 5 × $36,000 = **$510,000**.

The $204,019 gross benefit uses the fictional customer work pattern and the March 2026 U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics production/material-moving total-compensation benchmark of $36.27 per hour. A real offer must use observed productive hours, actual payroll burden, retained work, availability, and contract terms.

## Manufacturer break-even screen

- Unit contribution screen = $285,000 selling price − $150,000 Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) ceiling = **$135,000 per unit**.
- Units to recover development authorization = $10,975,000 ÷ $135,000 = 81.30, rounded up to **82 units**.

That 82-unit figure excludes sales expense, warranty, service delivery, corporate overhead, working capital, tax, financing, and future engineering. It is a minimum screening hurdle, not a profit forecast.

## Sensitivity and gate decision

| Input change | Result | Gate consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Unit contribution falls to $100,000 | 110 units to recover development | Commercial gate tightens |
| Unit contribution rises to $170,000 | 65 units to recover development | Still requires qualified pipeline |
| Customer gross value falls 20% | $127,215 annual net after service; 2.59-year payback | Customer screen still under three years |
| Customer gross value falls 40% | $86,411 annual net after service; 3.82-year payback | Fails a three-year customer screen |

Leadership therefore approved limited availability only. General Availability (GA) requires qualified demand, production evidence, second-source readiness, and fully burdened unit economics.
